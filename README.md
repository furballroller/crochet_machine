This is a convenient setup I found that helps with crochet. It can be useful for absolute beginners, lessening the learning curve, or possibly for advanced crocheters who may find relief from hand strain. I've also noticed that tension is quite even when the contraption manages it, so maybe this could be a good quality-control device (I'm not experienced enough at crochet to tell)

Crochet involves pulling yarn in three different directions:

-  LH pointer and pinky managing tension
-  LH thumb and index holding the work
-  RH hook pulling loops open

This contraption aims to manage the LH pointer's job, so crocheters have an easier time.

I decided to use laser cutting, since it's cool (and maybe better for the environment than 3D printing). Melbourne Makerspaces have free-to-use laser cutting machines, and Plyco offers free laserply samples that they accept. This is how I got the materials.

The contraption works with nuts and bolts clamping the wood to a table, and eye hooks holding yarn from the skein. A mass hangs on the yarn by a paperclip, providing consistent tension and reeling in any slack. The way the yarn is threaded through means there is more friction at the ball than at the crocheter's end, so yarn is pulled from the ball only when the crocheter runs out of yarn stored at the mass.
